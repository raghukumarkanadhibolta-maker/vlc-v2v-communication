🏛️ End-to-End System Architecture

This visible light communication (VLC / Li-Fi) vehicular network architecture implements dynamic link adaptation across physical optical channels. The transmitter (Car A) integrates perception sensors, ML channel sensing, Rate 1/2 Convolutional FEC encoding, and optical intensity modulation. The receiver (Car B) combines an optical concentrator, PIN photodiode, coherent RF matched filtering, Soft Viterbi Trellis decoding, CRC-16 framing, and acoustic-visual buzzer alerts.

[Car A Perception LiDAR] → [Hazard Detection: Pothole / Animal] → [ML Channel Classifier] → [Modulation Selector: OOK vs BPSK vs QPSK] → [Rate 1/2 FEC Encoder] → [LED Headlight (Tx)] → [Optical Vehicular Channel: Path Loss + Weather Attenuation + Misalignment] → [Car B Photodiode (Rx)] → [DC Glare Rejection Filter] → [Soft-Decision Viterbi Decoder] → [CRC-16 Integrity Check] → [Acoustic Dual-Tone Buzzer & Roof Warning Strobe]

Key Hardware & Signal Processing Layers

  • Optical Source: Automotive high-intensity LED headlights emitting in the visible spectrum (λ = 400–700 nm).
  • Optical Receiver: Silicon PIN photodiode with optical bandpass filter and compound parabolic concentrator (CPC).
  • Channel Estimation & ML Sensing: Pre-reception physical feature extraction (distance, ambient lux, radiation angle, incidence angle, obstacle status).
  • Forward Error Correction (FEC): Rate 1/2 Convolutional Code with constraint length K=3, generator polynomials g0=[1,1,1]_2=7_8, g1=[1,0,1]_2=5_8, and 4-state Soft-Decision Viterbi trellis decoding providing +4.5 dB coding gain.
  • Safety Compliance: End-to-end latency < 20.0 ms satisfying SAE J2735 / IEEE 802.11p equivalent vehicular active safety requirements.

📡 Three Modulation Techniques: Principles & Optical IM/DD Compatibility

Visible Light Communication requires optical Intensity Modulation and Direct Detection (IM/DD), because photodetectors measure only the instantaneous optical photon power (energy), not the optical phase or frequency. Therefore, all optical transmitted signals must be strictly non-negative: P_opt(t) ≥ 0.

1. OOK + FEC (On-Off Keying • Baseband IM/DD)

The simplest and most direct optical modulation technique. Bit 0 turns the LED output to minimum power (or 0), and Bit 1 pulses the LED to peak power. With Rate 1/2 Convolutional FEC, information bits are protected against random noise bursts.
Advantages: Near-instantaneous baseband latency (0.53 ms), zero RF mixer overhead, high peak throughput (1.0 Mbps uncoded, 500 kbps coded).
Limitations: Highly susceptible to ambient DC sunlight glare, low optical efficiency, requires high optical SNR (> 12 dB) to avoid packet drops.

2. BPSK + FEC (Subcarrier Intensity Modulation BPSK • SIM-BPSK)

In SIM-BPSK, digital bits modulate an intermediate radio frequency (RF) electrical subcarrier: s(t) = P_avg • [1 + m • b(t) • cos(2πf_c t)], where b(t) ∈ {-1, +1} and m ≤ 1.0 is the optical modulation index.
Advantages: The coherent RF matched filter at the receiver completely rejects DC ambient sunlight glare. Antipodal signaling provides a +3.0 dB electrical SNR advantage over unipolar OOK. Soft Viterbi decoding contributes +4.5 dB coding gain, providing 100% packet reliability in degraded channels.
Trade-off: Subcarrier filtering adds ~10 ms latency and 15% bandwidth overhead (434.8 kbps net throughput).

3. QPSK + FEC (Subcarrier Intensity Modulation QPSK • SIM-QPSK)

SIM-QPSK modulates two orthogonal subcarriers (In-phase and Quadrature) within the instantaneous LED drive current:
P_opt(t) = P_avg • [1 + m • (I_k • cos(2πf_c t) - Q_k • sin(2πf_c t))].
Each symbol transmits 2 encoded bits: (b_2k, b_2k+1) → (I_k, Q_k) ∈ {(±1/√2, ±1/√2)}.
Key Breakthrough: Transmits twice as many bits per symbol as BPSK, doubling the spectral efficiency from 0.435 to 0.870 bps/Hz and increasing net throughput to 869.6 kbps while retaining full DC sunlight rejection!

Viterbi Soft Metric Extraction

At the photodetector, direct detection photocurrent is AC-coupled to strip DC ambient light and LED bias. In-phase and Quadrature coherent matched filters yield:
y_I = I_k + n_I(t),   y_Q = Q_k + n_Q(t),   where n_I, n_Q ~ N(0, σ^2).
Soft metrics for the Rate 1/2 Viterbi decoder are extracted as:
soft_0 = √2 • y_I,   soft_1 = √2 • y_Q.
This directly feeds into the Soft-Decision Viterbi Trellis decoder, ensuring 100% fair coding gain comparisons against OOK and BPSK.

🌦️ Real-World Environmental & Vehicular Road Scenarios

Our simulation engine models optical wireless propagation under 10 independently configurable real-world road and weather conditions:

  • A. Clear Road / Clear Weather: Standard atmospheric visibility, negligible optical extinction (γ = 0.0001 dB/m). Stable line-of-sight communication between vehicles.
  • B. Sunlight / Daytime Glare: Direct sunlight up to 85,000 lux. Introduces strong DC photocurrent shot noise (σ_shot^2 = 2q I_bg B). Causes ~3.2 dB degradation on unipolar OOK; subcarrier BPSK and QPSK completely reject DC ambient glare.
  • C. Rainy Conditions: Light (2.5 mm/h), Moderate (12.5 mm/h), and Heavy Rain (25 mm/h). Modeled using the Carbonneau optical extinction relationship (γ_rain = a • R^b) combined with raindrop optical scattering.
  • D. Foggy Conditions: Light (V=500m), Moderate (V=200m), and Heavy Fog (V=50m). Modeled using the Kruse-Kim particle diffusion model, causing severe optical attenuation up to 8.0 dB at short vehicular distances.
  • E. Night-Time Conditions: Low ambient illuminance (2 lux). Noise floor is dominated exclusively by thermal noise. Headlight and taillight beams operate at maximum optical contrast, boosting SNR by +2.5 dB.
  • F. Pothole Ahead: Car A onboard perception detects a severe road depression, generating the emergency warning: "POTHOLE AHEAD - SLOW DOWN" and transmitting it to trailing vehicles.
  • G. Animal on Road: Car A LiDAR detects an animal crossing, generating the emergency alert: "ANIMAL AHEAD - REDUCE SPEED".
  • H. Vehicle Blockage & Multi-Hop Relay: Intermediate vehicle (Car C) blocks direct optical line-of-sight (> 25 dB blockage drop → packet outage). When Multi-hop Relay is enabled, Car C serves as a Decode-and-Forward relay node, recovering the link at the cost of 2-hop latency (+0.55 ms).
  • I. Vehicle Speed Scenarios (0, 30, 60, 90, 120 km/h): Higher vehicle speeds induce road roughness and chassis pitch/yaw vibration, increasing pointing misalignment (σ_θ up to 3.8°) and causing optical geometric loss.
  • J. Additional Traffic Scenarios: Sudden braking, emergency vehicle approaching, and multi-vehicle platoon chain communication.

System Architecture & Technology Stack

  • Backend API: Python 3.13, FastAPI 0.142, Uvicorn asynchronous ASGI server.
  • Physical Simulation Engine: NumPy Monte Carlo transceiver simulator with Rate 1/2 Convolutional FEC & Soft Viterbi Decoding.
  • Machine Learning Layer: Scikit-Learn Random Forest channel quality classifier trained on optical wireless measurement datasets.
  • Frontend UI/UX: Vanilla HTML5, CSS3 Glassmorphism, 2D HTML5 Canvas rendering for live dynamic research plots and atmospheric weather overlays.
  • Acoustic Alert: Web Audio API synthesized dual-tone emergency buzzer alarm (960 Hz / 1280 Hz).
  • Deployment: Multi-target production ready for Cloudflare Global Edge, Render, Railway, Vercel, and Docker.
